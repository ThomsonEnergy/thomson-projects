Overheads

What it really costs to run the business, so job costs include their share of it and the month-by-month profit is the real figure. Finance and Admin only.

How it works
Employee costs (super, workers comp, payroll tax, tools, vehicles...) are divided by the paid hours of the people who bill their time, and added to their hourly cost: their loaded rate.
Company costs (rent, insurance, accounting, software, marketing, plus all non-billable wages) are shared across jobs as a percentage of each job's profit.
Job pages then show costs including these overheads. The monthly summary at the bottom is the company-level result.

1. People and hours

Hours a year each person is paid for. Anyone who is not billable (office or admin staff) has their whole wage counted as a company cost instead. Leave the hours blank to use the default.

2. Employee direct costs

On top of wages, per year. Divided by the paid hours above and added to each billable person's hourly cost.

3. Company direct costs

Running the business, per year. Do not add materials, or the wages of people already costed against jobs. Non-billable wages are worked out for you from timesheets and admin pay (last line), so do not add wages here or map a wages account from Xero.

4. How company costs are shared across jobs

Each job carries a percentage of its own profit towards company costs: yearly company costs divided by the profit you expect the business to make in a year.

5. Keep it live from Xero

Pick which expense accounts on your Xero Profit and Loss are employee costs or company costs. Their yearly amounts are refreshed from Xero every night. Xero lines are read only here; change them by changing the mapping. Leave wage accounts as "Ignore".

6. Month by month

The company result each month, estimated from invoices sent, materials received, timesheets, and the yearly overhead figures divided by 12.
