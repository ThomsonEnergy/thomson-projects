Overheads

What it really costs to run the business, so job costs include their share of it and the month-by-month profit is the real figure. Finance and Admin only.

How it works
Billable hours = the hours each person is paid for, less leave, public holidays, training and their non-billable (admin) time. Overheads are recovered on billable hours only.
Direct employee overhead is each billable person's own on-costs: super, workers comp, payroll tax, leave loading, paid leave, public holidays and training. Divided by their billable hours and added to their pay rate: their true cost per hour.
Indirect employee overhead is all non-billable wage time: office and admin staff in full, plus everyone's non-job admin time. Spread across every billable hour.
Company costs are non-wage costs (insurance, vehicles, rent, software...), shared across jobs as a percentage of each job's profit.
Job pages show costs including all three. Each job's labour overhead uses the true cost of whoever logged the hours.

1. People and hours

Defaults for everyone. Leave a person's box blank to use the default; leave days are full-time days and are worked out pro rata for anyone on fewer days a week.

Subcontractors get no leave, public holidays, training or on-costs, but their billable hours still carry the indirect and company overheads. Other costs are anything for one person only (their phone, tools, licences), per year.

2. Employee on-costs

Percentages of each employee's wages. Part of each billable person's direct overhead (and of the indirect overhead for office and admin staff).

Other employee costs

Per year, shared by each employee's share of wages: uniforms, PPE, staff training courses, staff amenities. Super, workers comp and payroll tax are covered by the percentages above, so do not add them here or map them from Xero.

3. Indirect employee overhead

Worked out for you from section 1: every non-billable wage hour.

4. Company costs

Non-wage costs of running the business, per year: insurance, vehicles and fuel, rent, accounting, software, marketing, phones and internet. No wages here (they are all in sections 1 to 3) and no job materials.

5. How company costs are shared across jobs

Each job carries a percentage of its own profit towards company costs: yearly company costs divided by the profit you expect the business to make in a year.

6. Keep it live from Xero

Pick which expense accounts on your Xero Profit and Loss are other employee costs or company costs. Their yearly amounts are refreshed from Xero every night. Xero lines are read only here; change them by changing the mapping. Leave wages, super, workers comp and payroll tax accounts as "Ignore": they are worked out above.

7. Month by month

The company result each month, estimated from invoices sent, materials received, timesheets, and the yearly overhead figures divided by 12.
