# Solar quote compliance requirements: SAA, NETCC and STC

This lists every requirement I could find, pulled directly from the primary sources (the actual Code, the actual Clean Energy Regulator guideline, the actual sample STC form), not summaries of summaries. It is organised by which body actually imposes the requirement, since they are three separate things that overlap in practice:

1. **SAA (Solar Accreditation Australia)** individual accreditation of the designer/installer. Took over this function from the Clean Energy Council (CEC) in 2024. Only an SAA accredited person's design/install work is eligible to create STCs.
2. **NETCC (New Energy Tech Consumer Code)** a consumer protection code the business signs up to. It governs what a quote and contract must say, sales conduct, warranties, complaints and payment terms. It is voluntary but Thomson Energy has presumably signed up given the question, so it is binding on the business.
3. **STC (Small-scale Technology Certificates)**, administered by the Clean Energy Regulator (CER). These are the certificates that produce the discount customers see on their quote. Creating them legally requires specific information to be collected and specific written statements to be obtained, from the retailer, the installer, the designer and (if different) the electrician.

There is a fourth, separate thing worth knowing about even though it is not what was asked: **CEC Approved Solar Retailer** is a different, voluntary scheme at the business level (5 year minimum warranty, financial/integrity checks, CEC accredited installers only). It is not the same as NETCC, though the two overlap heavily in intent. Worth a look separately if the business wants that badge, but it is not covered further below.

## 1. NETCC: what every quote must contain

Source: the New Energy Tech Consumer Code itself (September 2020 version, currently live at newenergytech.org.au). Clause numbers below match the Code so anyone can go back to the original text.

**General quote requirements (clause 9):**
- Full business name, ABN, physical address, email/electronic address, phone number
- Itemised list of what is being supplied: manufacturer, model, year, quantities, configuration and performance specs for every product (panels, inverter, battery)
- Information about how the system operates
- Any responsibilities the customer has (maintenance, access for future service)
- Any limitations that are likely to matter to that customer (e.g. "this battery has no backup function")
- A performance estimate, reasonably based
- A statement that their energy retailer contract may change as a result of installing solar, and that it is the customer's responsibility to check this with their retailer
- The timeframe for supply and installation, and anything outside Thomson Energy's control that could delay it
- Payment method/terms
- Full warranty details: that the contractual warranty is on top of (not instead of) Australian Consumer Law guarantees, the specific warranty terms, and the name and contact details of the product supplier (so the customer can pursue a claim against them directly if needed)
- Expected product life and disposal/end of life information
- Portability of the system (relevant mainly for things like EV chargers, less so fixed solar)
- Term of any ongoing agreement (e.g. monitoring subscription) and anything that affects the customer's existing energy retailer relationship
- If it is a strata property: a statement that Owners Corporation written approval is needed before the contract is signed, and that the customer must get it and give it to Thomson Energy first
- **On the front page, prominently: cooling-off and termination rights** (10 business days if the sale was unsolicited, under both Australian Consumer Law and the Code)
- Any licences, accreditation or certification held that are relevant to the offer (this is where SAA/CEC accreditation numbers belong)
- A statement that Thomson Energy is bound by the Code
- The NETCC "Consumer Information Product" (a standard document explaining the Code, available from newenergytech.org.au)

**Financial disclosure (clauses 10-16):**
- Deposit amount (if any) and total price including all taxes
- How long the quoted price is valid for, minimum 10 business days
- Any site conditions that could cause extra chargeable work not covered by the quote (e.g. meter upgrade, fixing an existing fault)
- The total value of any discount, rebate or incentive, including the STC discount, and how/when it applies
- **If a return-on-investment or payback claim is made**, the quote must show the assumptions behind it: system design/output, incentives used, energy prices/usage assumed, financing costs, maintenance costs, end-of-life costs. Must be clearly labelled as an estimate that may not be achieved.

**Design requirements (clause 17), since almost every solar quote needs custom design:**
- A site-specific installation design or plan (a sketch/diagram is fine) including panel positioning and how it integrates with anything the customer already has
- A site-specific performance estimate
- These must be completed **before the contract is signed**, unless a non-refundable design fee is charged, or the customer is given a full refund right if they reject the design within 10 business days of receiving it

**Grid connection (clause 18), if relevant:**
- Whether Thomson Energy will arrange the retailer/network approval and meter reconfiguration, and any charge for doing so
- What paperwork is needed and by when
- A statement that the customer's energy retailer may charge for this or change their existing pricing as a result

**Contract requirements (clauses 19-23), once the customer accepts:**
- Everything from the quote, plus: the minimum warranty period (workmanship and performance), how to make a complaint, and the front page cooling-off notice again if it is an unsolicited sale
- Both parties sign (electronic acceptance is fine)

**Fit-for-purpose check before quoting at all (clause 7), i.e. what to ask the customer:**
- Their specific circumstances, needs and expectations, including whether they want to stay grid-connected, go off-grid, or participate in a virtual power plant
- Whether anyone at the property needs power for medical or life-support equipment (if so, must confirm the system is suitable and flag any added risk)
- Whether the new system needs to work with anything they already have (existing solar, existing battery)
- If they are considering going off-grid: give them the NETCC "Off-Grid Principles" consumer information document

**Other binding obligations worth staff training on, not quote fields as such:**
- No high-pressure sales tactics (clause 5), extra care with vulnerable customers (clause 6)
- Deposit receipts must always be issued (clause 24)
- If offering finance/deferred payment, specific disclosure rules apply (clause 25)
- Complaints must be acknowledged promptly and substantively responded to within 25 business days (clause 54)

## 2. STC eligibility: what must be collected and declared

Source: CER's "Small generation units: collecting mandatory information to create small-scale technology certificates" (systems installed from 1 April 2022) and CER's sample STC assignment form for solar/battery systems (June 2025, the current version).

**System and installation details to record for every job:**
- Installation date (the date it is complete and capable of generating)
- Whether a solar retailer was involved (yes for us) and if so: retailer legal entity name, retailer ABN, retailer representative's first name, last name and role (must match whoever signs the retailer statement below)
- National Metering Identifier (NMI) at the site, if it is on the National Electricity Market
- PV module brand and model for every distinct panel type used (must be on the CEC approved list)
- Inverter manufacturer, series and model for every inverter used
- System mounting type (on a building/structure, or ground mounted)
- Whether it is a complete unit, and installation type: new / replacement / extension / additional. If extension or additional, the position of the new panels relative to the existing system must be described (e.g. "east of existing array, on the shed roof")
- Deeming period selected (1 year, 5 years, or maximum)
- Rated power output in kW (of the panels, not the inverter)
- Number of panels and every individual panel serial number
- Number of inverters and every individual inverter serial number
- Grid connection type: grid-connected without battery, grid-connected with battery, or stand-alone
- If a battery is installed: battery manufacturer and model, nominal and usable capacity (kWh), number of batteries and their serial numbers, whether the installer changed the battery's default manufacturer settings, and whether it is (or is capable of being) part of a Virtual Power Plant

**Site and owner details:**
- Property type (residential/commercial/school), single or multi storey
- Full installation address, plus GPS coordinates if the address alone would not pinpoint the system (rural properties especially)
- Whether more than one small generation unit is installed at the same address
- System owner's full details: name, whether individual or a company (company name, ACN/ABN, position of the signer), phone, email, postal address

**People who must be identified with their accreditation numbers, for every job:**
- **Installer**: full name, SAA accreditation number, accreditation type (grid-connected or stand-alone), company name, phone, email, address
- **Designer** (if different to the installer): same details plus their SAA accreditation number
- **Electrician** (if different again, e.g. for the AC-side wiring): full name, electrical licence number, company, contact details

**Written statements that must be obtained and kept on file (these are legal declarations, not just data fields):**
- **From the installer**: confirms they held the correct accreditation, complied with its requirements, that all local/state safety and technical compliance documentation exists and was given to the customer, that the design was followed (or any modification was itself compliant), that the panel and inverter models used were CEC-approved and not subject to any recall, and (for grid-connected systems) that the inverter met AS/NZS 4777.2. They must also declare they were on-site to install or personally supervise the installation.
- **From the designer**: confirms their identity, accreditation, and that the design complies with the accreditation scheme's requirements.
- **From Thomson Energy as the retailer**: confirms who installed the job and their employment relationship with us; that the system will perform per the contract/quote; that it is complete and generating (or capable of); that it is connected to the grid or that we have met our obligations for that connection; that we gave the customer written information on feed-in tariffs, export limits, and at least one of expected payback period, energy savings or cost savings, and that this information was true and complete; and a conflicts-of-interest disclosure statement.
- **From the system owner**: a mandatory declaration that they own the system, have not previously assigned or created STCs for it, understand how many STCs it is eligible for and what they are receiving in exchange for assigning that right, and that they understand giving false information is an offence. This needs the owner's (and a witness's) signature and date.
- For a battery specifically, the retailer statement additionally covers: that the property already has solar PV and the battery is appropriately sized for it; whether it is VPP-capable; and written disclosure of appropriate battery/inverter sizing relative to the existing system and the property's electricity needs.

All of the above must be retained as records (CER can audit), and every written statement's exact wording matters (they carry legal penalties for false or misleading information under the Renewable Energy (Electricity) Act 2000 and the Criminal Code Act 1995), so these should be fixed, versioned text blocks in the quoting tool, not something a staff member free-types each time.

## 3. What this means for the quoting tool, in plain terms

Putting the two together, a solar quote should not be able to be finalised or sent until the following are captured, because without them either the quote itself is non-compliant with NETCC or the job cannot legally create STCs afterward:

**Before quoting (customer conversation, NETCC clause 7):**
- Grid-connected, off-grid, or VPP interest
- Anyone on-site needing power for medical/life-support equipment
- Any existing solar/battery system it needs to integrate with

**On the quote itself (NETCC):**
- Itemised equipment list with manufacturer/model for every panel and inverter (and battery if included)
- Site-specific design sketch/diagram and site-specific performance estimate
- Full warranty terms plus the product supplier's own contact details
- Deposit amount, total price including GST, and the exact STC/rebate value being applied
- Quote validity period (must state at least 10 business days)
- Cooling-off/termination rights, shown prominently on the front page
- If a payback/ROI figure is quoted: the assumptions behind it, stated explicitly
- SAA accreditation number(s) of the installer/designer who will do the job

**For STC eligibility (must exist before or at installation, not retrofitted later):**
- NMI, exact installation address (plus GPS if rural)
- Every panel and inverter serial number, and (if fitted) every battery serial number
- Installer's SAA number, designer's SAA number if different, electrician's licence number if different
- The full retailer, installer, designer and owner written statements, signed and dated, using fixed compliant wording, not free text
- Confirmation the panels and inverter model are on the current CEC approved product list at time of installation (this changes over time, worth a periodic check rather than a one-off)

Making the last group mandatory before a job can be marked complete/invoiced is probably the highest-value change: it is the one most likely to actually cause a rejected STC claim (real money) if missed, since CER audits against exactly this list.
